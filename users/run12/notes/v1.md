## What shipped
Your spending, broken down by category, for the last 30 days.

The main panel is a pie: one slice per category, biggest to smallest, each one
labelled with its name, what it came to, and its share of the total. Every
category you spent in is there — nothing is filtered down to a shortlist.

Underneath it is the full list of transactions behind that picture, newest
first, so you can check any slice against what actually went through. Each row
shows which account it came from and when.

You can also shape the categories yourself:

- **Make your own category** and move any transaction into it. The move sticks
  — it survives every future refresh, so you only ever have to do it once per
  transaction.
- **Tick a category off** to take it out of the pie and out of the
  percentages. It stays in the list with its own box, so you can put it back
  whenever you like.

Both your checking account and your credit card feed the same picture rather
than being split into two, and the panel names the accounts it is counting so
you can always see what is included.

Your data refreshes when you press Refresh at the top of the screen, and at no
other time. The last-updated time sits right next to that button. If your bank
doesn't answer, the screen says so rather than quietly showing you yesterday's
figures as though they were today's.

## Built differently
**Two things about how the picture is put together.**

Each slice is labelled beside the pie rather than on top of it. Written across
the wedges, the smaller labels came out too faint to read reliably against
their own colours, so every category gets a proper line next to the chart with
its name, its amount and its percentage — which also means the small slices are
just as readable as the big ones.

Transfers between your own accounts, card payments, and money coming in are
left out of the percentages by default, and the panel tells you how many it
left out. You asked to have this decided during the build. Left in, moving
money from checking to savings reads as though you had spent it, and paying off
your card counts the same spending twice. Every one of those transactions is
still in the list underneath, marked, and you can move any of them into a real
category if you disagree with the call.

## Notes for the next build
**Nothing is unbuilt, which is why `## Open` above is empty rather than saying
so.** `scripts/announce-deploy.ts` warns Nico whenever that section has any
non-whitespace in it, so "nothing to report" written out longhand is a false
alarm at the moment he is standing at a terminal after a deploy. Every change
spec v1 asked for is built, and its three open questions are resolved in the
build or answered at link time.

**The one thing spec v1 flagged to watch.** Its second open question expects
the bank's own categories to possibly turn out too coarse or too noisy once he
is looking at real months, and names renaming or merging them as the likely
next step. He can already make his own categories and move transactions into
them, which covers most of it — but merging two bank categories into one still
means moving each transaction by hand. Not proposed, and not worth proposing
until he has seen a real month. Recorded in `current.md` under
`## Deliberately not included` so the chat agent does not float it early.

**The migration chain is 001–004.** 001 and 002 are the vendored Plaid
envelope, byte-for-byte, never edited. 003 is the two views. 004 adds run12's
own three tables and DROPs/recreates `spending_transactions`. Changing either
view again is a 005 that drops and recreates it — no data moves, which is the
whole point of storing the payload whole.

**`is_internal` never fires against synthetic data, and that is not a dead
path.** Every transfer in the recorded Sandbox fixture sits on a savings, CD or
money-market account, which the account allow-list excludes before the flag is
ever consulted. So the "N transfers left out" caption and the "not counted"
marker do not render in dev. In production a card payment from the connected
checking account will be in scope. `seed.py`'s docstring says this too. Do not
conclude the path is dead from looking at the dev screen — build-rules §9.7 in
its own direction.

**An override clears `is_internal`.** A transaction the friend re-filed by hand
counts, whatever Plaid called it, because a control that silently does nothing
for one class of row is a broken control. The cost is that re-filing a card
payment into a real category reintroduces the double-count. Two things make
that recoverable rather than silent: the list marks every uncounted row, and
the tick box removes a whole category in one press. Do not "fix" this without
replacing both.

**Slice colour is assigned by RANK, not by category**, and that is deliberate
and validated. The palette's colourblind-safety guarantee covers ADJACENT
SLOTS; on a pie sorted biggest-first, adjacent wedges are adjacent ranks, so
rank assignment is what makes the checked pair list the real one. Pinning a
colour to a category name instead puts an arbitrary permutation of slots side
by side, and one such pair fails the normal-vision floor outright. If a future
version wants stable per-category colour, the palette has to be re-validated
under `--pairs all` first — it will not pass at eight slots. `palette.ts` has
the numbers.

**Eight colours, and there must never be a ninth.** Past the eighth the tail
folds into a neutral "Other" and the panel names how many went in. A generated
or recycled hue is how a palette stops being checkable.

**The account scope is an allow-list** — every `credit` account plus every
`depository` account whose subtype is `checking`. A bank reporting a current
account under some other subtype drops out silently, which is why the panel
names the accounts it counted. Widening it is a one-line view change in a new
migration with no data moved.

**The route is run12's own and is a near-twin of run11's on purpose.**
`app/api/users/[user]/spending-breakdown/route.ts` duplicates
`spending-category/route.ts` almost exactly. Do not merge them: a shared
handler makes a change to one friend's dashboard a silent change to another's,
which is the call `pee-log/route.ts` already made against reusing `pee`. The
restated `MAX_NAME` is pinned against `queries.ts`'s `CATEGORY_NAME_MAX` by
`tests/routing/spendingBreakdownRoute.test.ts`, which is what keeps the
duplication honest.

**`categoryTotals` returns a `total` that is only ever a denominator.** Nothing
renders it. The friend never asked for a grand total, so adding one is a
product decision, not a wiring-up.

**Two captions, kept disjoint by amount rather than by tick state.** A category
that netted away and a category he unticked are opposite facts and must not
share a sentence. A netted-away category also defaults to unticked, so
splitting on `included` alone makes one row produce both captions — or neither.
That was a real defect during the build, caught by rendering the screen rather
than by any test.

**Seed data has to be plausible as well as loudly fake.** The first version of
`seed_annotations` re-filed whichever transaction sorted first and produced a
four-figure "eating out" wedge at over half the pie; the second unticked a
category that was already off by default, so it demonstrated nothing. Both
passed every test. Both were found by rendering the synthetic database and
reading it.

**The content column is narrower than it looks.** The container is fluid to
1200px, but the chat panel takes 400–600px at desktop widths, so the real
width at 1440 is closer to 780px. The pie is a fixed square that fits at 375px
and the legend reflows around it; a `ResponsiveContainer` would render nothing
in the server HTML and jump on settle.
