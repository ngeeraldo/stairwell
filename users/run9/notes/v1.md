## What shipped
A pee tracker, on one screen that works the same on a phone and a computer.

Today's count sits at the top as a single large number, with a big button
under it that logs one more. Each tap counts on its own — tap it as many times
as you go. Underneath that is a small fix control: −1 takes back the most
recent one if you fumbled it, +1 adds one, and it only ever touches today.

Below the count, a bar chart of the last seven days so you can see whether
today is running high or low, and under that the average per day over the
week, drawn onto the chart as a dashed line so the two read together rather
than as two separate numbers.

The time of each tap is recorded quietly and shown nowhere, as agreed.

## Built differently
**The weekly average leaves today out.** You asked for a baseline to read the
daily trend against, and today is a day you are still adding to — an average
that counted it would sink every morning and climb through the afternoon, so
today would be partly compared against itself. It averages the completed days
instead, and says underneath how many it used.

**The trend starts when you do.** In the first week it shows only the days
since your first log rather than padding out to seven — days before the
tracker existed are not days you logged nothing, so they are left out rather
than drawn as zeros. A day you skip AFTER starting does show as zero, because
that one is real.

**The fix control sits inside the count panel** rather than in a panel of its
own. It belongs to the number directly above it, and keeping it there is what
lets the count, the log button and the fix all sit on the first screen of a
phone without scrolling.

## Notes for the next build
**The interview said "weekly trend"; the spec resolved it to a weekly
average.** Built to the spec, but the gap is worth knowing: if he asks again
for a "weekly trend" he may mean week-over-week, which is a second chart and
not this number. Do not treat a repeat request as him forgetting.

**`pee_logs.day` is decided at WRITE time and stored — never recompute it from
`at`.** The route resolves the friend's day from the browser's zone and files
the row under it, which is the settled pattern (friend-timezone ledger T1/T4).
Deriving the day from `at` at read time would silently re-file old rows the
first time he logs from another country, and every panel would need the
widen-and-filter dance `users/devone/queries.ts` does. It is also why every
function in `queries.ts` takes a day key as a parameter and touches no zone at
all — the only zone-sensitive step in this dashboard is the route, so the
timezone assertion lives in `tests/routing/peeRoute.test.ts` rather than in
this folder's own suite.

**`001_initial.sql` is frozen the moment this branch merges.** A fix is `002`
plus a data-survival test in the same commit.

**`TrendChart.tsx` is the first client component under `users/`, and
`tests/users/noLocalDay.test.ts` does not see it.** That sweep names
`dashboard.tsx` and `queries.ts`; a third `.tsx` in a user folder is unswept.
This one reads no clock — every day, count and label arrives resolved from
`queries.ts` — but nothing enforces that. Widening the sweep to
`users/*/*.tsx` would close it.

**The chart guard is what makes Recharts allowed here at all** (Nico's ruling,
2026-08-19: data-computing components are a sanctioned exception to
host-elements-only, guarded by the states rule). `chartable` in `dashboard.tsx`
must keep gating it, and `users/run9/tests/dashboard.test.ts` is what holds
that. The ruling names "all-identical" as degenerate; this build reads that as
`max === 0`, because a zero-based bar domain of `[0, 6]` charts a week of
identical days perfectly well and blanking it would hide a plausible week.

**Whether Recharts reaches other friends' pages is unverified.**
`.next/app-build-manifest.json` attributes its 364K chunk to `/[user]/page`,
which every friend loads. That is a static over-approximation and devone's RSC
payload should not reference the client module — but CLAUDE.md's
"nobody else's page carries it" is currently resting on that reasoning rather
than on a measurement.

**The count does not animate when it changes.** Every control is a
`<form method="post">` like the rest of the app, so a tap is a full round trip
and the render cannot tell "arrived after a POST" from "reloaded". The
guidelines' pop-on-change needs a client-side counter, which would cost the
no-JavaScript path. Deliberate, not forgotten.

**Test gotcha that cost half an hour:** `lib/session/keymap.ts` zeroes the key
buffer it holds when an entry ages out. A route test that hands `putKey` its
own key constant, then moves a fake clock past `IDLE_TTL_MS`, gets its constant
wiped — surfacing as "file is not a database" from a decrypt several lines
later, nowhere near the clock change. Hand it a copy.
