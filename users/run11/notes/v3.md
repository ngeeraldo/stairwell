## What shipped
A third tab, **Spending**, alongside the two dog screens. Nothing on those two
changed.

At the top, **Where it went**: a pie of the last 30 days from your credit card
and your debit card, one slice per category, with every category listed beside
it carrying its dollar amount and its share of the period. No grand total
anywhere — you said you'd misspoken about that and meant the amount per
category, so that's what it shows.

Under it, **Transactions**: the last 30 days, each with its date, what it was,
the amount, and the category it currently sits in. Two things you can do there:

- **Move any transaction into a different category.** It sticks — future
  refreshes from your bank won't undo it — and the pie above redraws with it,
  so the two never disagree about where a dollar sits.
- **Make your own categories.** Type a name and it becomes available to move
  things into; it shows up as its own slice once anything is filed there.

Each category in the list beside the pie also has a **tick box**. Untick one and
it drops out of the pie *and* out of the percentages, so everything else
rescales — but its transactions stay in the list below, and you can tick it back
any time. That's there for the things that aren't really spending: moving money
between your own accounts, or paying off the card from your current account,
which would otherwise sit there as one of your biggest "categories".

Money coming back — a refund, a credit — counts against the category it came
back from rather than being ignored, so a flight you booked and cancelled
doesn't read as money you spent on travel.

Your bank data updates **when you press Refresh and at no other time.** Nothing
can pull it on your behalf while you're away, so the screen says so rather than
letting you assume it's live.

## Built differently
**The amounts and percentages sit in the list beside the pie rather than being
written across the slices.** You asked to see the amount by category
"somewhere", and this is that — but with seven or eight categories, labels
written onto the slices themselves collide with each other and become
unreadable on a narrow window, and the smallest real slice is well under one
percent. The list beside the chart gives every category its name, its amount and
its share on one line, at any width, and stays readable when a category is tiny.

**Refunds and credits subtract from their category rather than being left out.**
The spec didn't say either way. Counting only money going out would say you
spent on a flight you were refunded for — and since the pie exists to answer
"what percentage of my money is going where", a number you got back would make
that percentage wrong. A category that nets to nothing draws no slice, but its
transactions stay in the list and its row stays on screen showing zero.

**The tick boxes weren't in the spec, and they're there because of something the
spec flagged.** A payment from your current account to your credit card is money
moving between two things you own, not money spent — and it's often large enough
to be the biggest slice on the screen, which would make the whole picture read
wrong. Rather than deciding for you which categories "aren't really spending",
the screen shows everything and lets you take things out. Categories that net to
nothing start unticked, since there's nothing to draw for them either way.

## Open
**The transfers question the spec raised is now his to fix, but he has to
notice it first.** Spec v3's second open question — whether refunds, credits,
transfers between his own accounts, or card payments from the debit account
should be excluded — is answered by the tick boxes rather than by a rule. Two
caveats worth a sentence from you:

- Netting only cancels a transfer whose OTHER SIDE is also connected. A card
  payment cancels itself once both the card and the current account are in;
  a transfer to an account this screen doesn't cover has nothing to cancel
  against and lands as a slice.
- So on first load he may see a large "loan payments" or "transfer" slice that
  is his own card payment, and nothing on screen tells him that it is. The
  control is discoverable; the *reason he'd want it* is not. This is the one
  thing worth putting into his chat rather than leaving him to work out.

**Spec v3's first open question is only half answered.** "What the first-load
state looks like if only one of the two is connected" — the screen names the
accounts it is counting, so a missing one is visible rather than silent, and it
offers to connect another. It does not say "you have one of two connected",
because nothing in the data says how many he intends to connect. Worth
confirming that naming the accounts is enough.

**The account scope is an allow-list and could silently drop his debit card.**
The screen counts every credit account plus every depository account whose
subtype is `checking`. If his bank reports the debit account under some other
depository subtype, it drops out of the picture — visibly, because the panel
names what it counts, but he'd have to read that line to notice. Widening it is
a one-line view change in a `005`. The alternative, every depository account,
pulls savings and money-market transfers into a spending pie, which is the
problem above made worse.

**Carried forward, unchanged by this build:** v1 and v2's note that the walk
verdict is "as of" the last refresh rather than live, which is still a platform
contract change (`now` on `DashboardProps`) rather than a dashboard one; and
v2's note that a calendar week starting on Sunday was my choice and has never
been confirmed. Flagging both again rather than letting them go quiet.

## Notes for the next build
**`003_module_plaid_initial.sql` is a VERBATIM COPY of
`modules/plaid/initial.sql` and a test asserts it byte-for-byte.** That is the
never-forked rule made checkable: an edit here would still apply cleanly and the
manifest would still match, because the manifest hashes what is on disk. Anything
this friend needs that Plaid's shape does not give him goes in a LATER migration
as views over the stored JSON — which is what `004` is.

**`003` and `004` are both frozen the moment this deploys.** A fix is `005`
plus a data-survival test in the same commit; `users/run11/tests/migrationV3.test.ts`
is the worked example and asserts whole rows rather than counts, because a
migration that dropped and recreated a table would keep the count and lose the
contents. `004` was still editable during this build only because it had never
reached `main` and his real database had never seen it — that window is closed
now.

**The visibility default is resolved at READ time and must never be
materialised.** `category_visibility` holds only choices he has actually
pressed; a category with no row is ticked if it nets positive and unticked if it
does not. Writing a default row when one is first rendered would be a render
writing to his database, which the read-only handle forbids outright — but the
behavioural reason matters more: a category that nets to nothing this fortnight
and goes positive next month comes back on its own, instead of staying silently
switched off because of one quiet spell he was not watching. Both directions are
tested, including the mirror case where an explicit untick survives the amount
moving.

**It stores a BOOLEAN, not a presence, and that is forced by the conditional
default.** He must be able to untick something that would default on AND tick
something that would default off. One column of presence can only express one of
those.

**`category_visibility` is case-SENSITIVE while `custom_categories` is
`COLLATE NOCASE`, and the route's case-snap is what keeps them aligned.** The
NOCASE key stops two buckets differing only in case; the case-sensitive key
holds a category KEY out of the view's COALESCE, and those keys are compared
exactly everywhere else — so folding them here would give one tick box to two
slices the pie draws separately. The route snaps an incoming category name to
the bucket's stored spelling before writing either table. Not reachable through
the dashboard, whose menu posts the stored spelling; reachable through the no-JS
path, which posts whatever the form holds. Do not remove it.

**There is deliberately NO foreign key from `transaction_category_overrides` to
`plaid_transactions`.** The sync stream's `removed` verb deletes rows and Plaid
can re-send a transaction it previously removed. A cascade would destroy his
re-filing at the moment his bank restated something — precisely when he would
not be looking — and a RESTRICT would make the refresh route fail on a delete it
is required to perform. An override with no matching transaction is inert. There
is a test for the remove-and-re-send round trip.

**Every rule that decides what a slice IS lives in `queries.ts`, not in the
components.** `SpendingPie.tsx` and `CategoryControls.tsx` are client
components, so their bodies never run in this folder's own vitest suite — the
same limitation `lib/ui/useWriteAction.ts` records about its own guard. A rule
left in either would be a rule nothing here can check. They map props to markup
and decide nothing.

**The chart's states check is in `dashboard.tsx`, not inside `SpendingPie.tsx`,
and it has to be.** A guard inside the component would run after it had already
mounted, which is the thing the component rule forbids. The caller proves there
is at least one slice and that every amount is finite and positive before
mounting anything.

**This folder is the repo's first Recharts consumer and its first
`useWriteAction` consumer.** Two things to know: the pie is drawn at a fixed
square rather than in a `ResponsiveContainer`, because the container measures on
the client and would render nothing in the server HTML and then jump — a circle
has nothing to gain from being fluid. And `CategoryControls.tsx` is built on the
hook rather than on `WriteAction` because a menu, a text field and a tick box
are not labelled buttons; the write LIFETIME is still platform code and is not
reimplemented there.

**The palette is validated and must not grow an eighth colour.** Seven
categorical slots cleared every adjacent-pair gate against this app's light
surface; past the seventh, the remainder folds into a neutral "Other". A
generated or recycled hue is how a palette stops being checkable. Three of the
seven sit below 3:1 contrast on the page, and the relief for that is the legend
beside the chart — it is the table view, not decoration, and it is also what
keeps a slice from being identified by colour alone. `users/run11/palette.ts`
carries the full result.

**The fold names what it swallowed, and every folded category keeps its own
legend row and tick box.** A fold that swallowed a category's control along with
its wedge would be silent truncation with an extra step.

**One route for all four spending actions, which is the OPPOSITE call from the
other two run11 routes.** `WriteAction` groups pending state by action URL, and
every control on this screen changes the same surface — the pie and the list are
drawn from one read and must never be seen disagreeing — so they should lock
together and settle together. The walk-log and no-go-temp routes stay separate
for the mirror-image reason.

**The route takes `show`/`hide`, never a toggle.** A toggle computes the new
state from what it finds, so two presses racing each other can land where
neither asked. Naming the target state makes a repeat idempotent — the same
reasoning as the no-go-temp route taking a direction, arrived at from the
opposite end.

**Free text arrives on this dashboard for the first time**, as a category name
he types. It is bounded, trimmed and whitespace-collapsed before it is stored —
the name is a PRIMARY KEY, and two spellings of one bucket are two rows he
cannot tell apart on screen. It lives in his own encrypted database and nowhere
else, and it NEVER reaches `metrics`: the route emits a constant panel name, and
the route test asserts the whole metric row and additionally that the typed name
appears nowhere in it. `MAX_NAME` is restated in the route rather than imported
from `queries.ts`, because a platform route importing a user folder would make
one friend's dashboard a build dependency of the platform; a test reads the
route's source and pins the number against the queries module.

**Sandbox signs one transaction the opposite way production would, and the dev
screen shows it.** The recorded fixture's card carries an automatic payment that
Plaid Sandbox signs as an outflow, so it lands as the largest slice locally. In
production a card payment arrives as an inflow on the card and cancels against
the outflow on the current account. Do NOT "fix" the netting based on what the
synthetic screen looks like — the arithmetic is right and the fixture is what is
unusual.

**`seed.py` seeds NO tick choices, deliberately.** The legend's boxes read from
`category_visibility`, which holds only real presses. Seeding a row would put a
choice in his mouth and would hide both of the things the dev screen most needs
to show: the zero-net category defaulting to unticked, and the dominating
payment slice in its raw first-load state. Unticking that slice on the dev
screen is also the cheapest way to watch the whole write path work end to end —
the same call the walk log makes by leaving today's square unmarked.

**Three products on one dashboard now, and they must not be joined.** The
decider reads no walk and no transaction, the log reads no forecast and no
transaction, and spending reads neither of the others. There are tests in both
directions. The obvious joins — "you walked on 4 of the 6 days it said don't
go", "you spend more on days you don't walk" — are the ones to propose out loud
rather than slip in.
