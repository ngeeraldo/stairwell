## What shipped
Two things, and the second one adds a second screen — so there is now a row of
tabs at the top: **Walk the dog?** (still where you land) and **Walk log**.

**Your own no-go temperature**, at the foot of the "Walk the dog?" screen. A
minus and a plus, one degree a press. The verdict and the next good window
both recompute the moment the server has it, in place, without the page
moving. You set only the hard-no number — the "short one, shade" band is
always the five degrees directly below whatever you pick, so 92 gives you
87–92. Until you touch it, it sits at 90°F and the screen behaves exactly as
it did.

**A Walk log screen**, for recording that a walk actually happened. Three
things on it:

- **Current streak** — consecutive days with a walk marked. If you have not
  marked today yet, it shows the run through yesterday and says so, rather
  than dropping to zero at midnight for a day that is not over.
- **Month calendar** — the walked days marked, and the place you mark them.
  One tap on a day's square marks it; tap it again to unmark, so a mis-tap
  costs nothing. Back-filling a day you missed is the same tap on that
  earlier square. A back arrow reaches earlier months — a full year, and
  further than that if you have history there. Days that have not happened
  yet are not tappable.
- **Days walked** — the share of days with a walk marked, as a percentage,
  with the count underneath it, because "18 of 30" and "3 of 5" are the same
  percentage and very different facts.

One walk a day is what gets recorded: no count of walks within a day, no
duration, no notes. Everything on this screen is what you entered — it reads
nothing from the forecast, and the decider screen reads nothing from it. With
nothing marked yet it says so in words rather than showing a confident zero.

## Built differently
**The percentage says "since you started" until the log is a month old.** The
window is the last 30 days, as discussed — but on your third day it reads
"2 of the 3 days since you started" rather than "2 of the last 30 days, 7%".
The days before the screen existed are days you could not have marked, and
counting them as days you skipped would make the number wrong in the one
direction that matters at the start.

**The temperature is a minus and a plus rather than a box to type in.** It
cannot produce a number that is not a whole degree, so there is nothing to get
wrong — and because both panels above it recompute on every press, nudging it
is how you find your number: the verdict flips under your hand at the degree
where it flips, instead of you guessing once and saving.

## Open
**Which day a calendar week starts on was never stated, and I chose Sunday.**
Nothing in the spec or the conversation says. Sunday is the US default and he
is in Houston, so that is what shipped — but it is his calendar and it is the
kind of thing people have a firm opinion about without ever thinking to
mention it. Worth asking. `WEEK_STARTS_ON` in `users/run11/queries.ts` is one
constant and the column headings derive from it, so Monday is a one-line
change with no component edit.

**The percentage window was spec v2's own open question and I settled it at 30
days.** The alternative on the table was "percentage of the month currently
shown on the calendar". Thirty days is the reading closest to the "18 of the
last 30 days" framing that prompted the panel, and it is the one that does not
change meaning when he pages the calendar back to look at July — but the spec
asked for it to be confirmed and only he can confirm it. It is on screen now,
labelled, so he may simply say.

**Still open from v1, unchanged: the verdict is "as of" the last refresh, not
live.** v1's note has the full reasoning. Nothing in v2 touched it — a
dashboard is still handed the day and never the time — and it is still a
platform decision (`now: number` on `DashboardProps`) rather than a dashboard
one. Flagging it again rather than letting it go quiet after one version.

## Notes for the next build
**`002_walk_log_and_settings.sql` is frozen the moment this deploys**, the same
way 001 already is. A fix is `003` plus a data-survival test in the same
commit — `users/run11/tests/migration002.test.ts` is the worked example, and it
asserts whole rows rather than counts, because a migration that dropped and
recreated a table would keep the count and lose the contents.

**The walk log and the forecast must not be joined.** Spec v2 is explicit that
the log "reads nothing from the forecast and shares no data with the decider",
and `users/run11/tests/dashboard.test.ts` asserts it in both directions. It
looks like an obvious future feature — "you walked on 4 of the 6 days it said
don't go" — and it is the thing to propose rather than to slip in, because it
turns a log of what he did into a scoreboard of when he ignored the dashboard.

**The month on screen is client state, and that is a known compromise.**
`users/run11/MonthCalendar.tsx` is the only client component this folder owns,
and it exists for one reason: `DashboardProps` carries `screen` and nothing
else from the URL, so there is no `?month=` a server render could read. The
cost is that the month is not bookmarkable and paging writes no
`dashboard_open` row. If `searchParams` is ever added to `DashboardProps`, this
file collapses into host elements in `dashboard.tsx` and the state moves into
the URL where the rest of this app keeps its navigation.

**Every calendar rule lives in `calendarGrid`, not in the component, and that
is deliberate.** A client component's body never runs in a per-dashboard vitest
suite — `lib/ui/useWriteAction.ts` records the same limitation about its own
guard — so a rule left in `MonthCalendar.tsx` would be a rule nothing in
`users/run11/tests/` can check. Which square is today, which is in the future
and therefore carries no control at all, and which is filled all come from
`queries.ts`. Keep it that way; the component maps cells to markup and decides
nothing.

**The pre-existence bound in `walkRate` is not a rounding detail.** The window
starts at the later of (today − 29) and his first mark. Collapsing it to a
fixed 30-day denominator is a one-line "simplification" that reintroduces
exactly the failure `docs/dashboard-build-rules.md` §6 is about — days before
the friend started rendering as days he failed. The first mark is the only
"when did this begin" signal there is, and a back-filled earlier day correctly
extends the window backwards.

**Two routes, not one, and the reason is `WriteAction`'s pending grouping.**
`lib/ui/WriteAction.tsx` locks every control sharing an action URL while a
write is in flight. The forty-odd calendar squares SHOULD lock together — they
write one table and the streak and percentage move with them — and the
temperature stepper should not be dragged into that. Merging
`app/api/users/[user]/walk-log/route.ts` and `.../no-go-temp/route.ts` would
be a visible regression, not a tidy-up.

**The walk-log route takes a `day` from the caller, which no other write route
in this repo does.** Back-filling requires it. That makes `day` the only
friend-supplied value any write route writes into a database, and it is a
PRIMARY KEY — a malformed one is not a bad row that can be fixed later, it is a
row nothing can ever address again. Three checks guard it: shape, a real-date
round trip (`2026-02-30` matches the pattern and rolls forward to March 2nd),
and not in the future, judged in the friend's own calendar. Do not relax any of
them, and do not let a second route grow the same habit without the same three.

**The no-go bounds are duplicated in the route on purpose.**
`app/api/users/[user]/no-go-temp/route.ts` restates `MIN_F`, `MAX_F`, `STEP_F`
and `DEFAULT_F` rather than importing them from `users/run11/queries.ts` — a
platform route importing a user folder would make one friend's dashboard a
build dependency of the platform. `users/run11/tests/noGoTemp.test.ts` reads
the route's source and pins the four numbers against the queries module, which
is what keeps the duplication honest. The range itself is NOT a CHECK in the
migration, deliberately: it is a product judgement, and freezing it into an
applied migration would make widening it a table rebuild.

**The route takes a direction, never a number.** `raise`/`lower`, computed from
the stored row inside one transaction. A body carrying a target would let two
presses each compute from a value the other had already replaced, and it would
make every bound something the caller chose to respect. `WriteAction` disables
its control mid-flight so the race is unreachable through the dashboard — but
the no-JS path has no such guard, and "the browser prevents it" is not where a
bound belongs.

**`markedDays` is unbounded on purpose.** A `LIMIT` would silently truncate a
long streak. The table holds at most one row per day he has ever walked, so a
decade is under four thousand short strings, and one read feeds all three
panels — three queries would be three overlapping reads and a third answer to
"what counts as marked".

**The day never goes into `metrics`.** `walk_log_mark` / `walk_log_unmark` and
`no_go_temp_raise` / `no_go_temp_lower` are constants; the day and the
temperature are not written anywhere unencrypted. This matters more on this
dashboard than on the others because the day is the one thing the caller sends,
and it is a fact about his life. Both route tests assert the row's whole
contents, and the walk-log one additionally asserts the day string appears
nowhere in it.

**v1's note said "two thresholds are load-bearing in different places" — one of
them is now his.** The verdict uses his no-go number and the derived shade
floor; the window scan uses only his no-go number, still deliberately, because
a short-one-and-shade stretch is a window worth naming. Rain and daylight are
unchanged and are not his to set: he was offered both heat bands and asked for
just the hard-no number, and nothing has ever been said about rain.

**Nothing animates a value when it lands.** The controls show their own
progress and every affected number patches in together when the server
confirms, which is the substantive half of the guidelines' animation rule. The
"pop" on a landed write is not built, because doing it per number would mean a
bespoke client component around each of them and no dashboard in this repo has
one. If it is ever wanted, it belongs in `lib/ui/` as a shared primitive, not
here.
