## What shipped
A "Walk the dog?" screen that answers the question in one word before you read
anything else: **Go**, **Go — short one, shade**, or **Don't go**. Under it, one
line saying what is against you — rain, heat, daylight, or more than one of
them at once.

Under that, the next good window: the next stretch of at least 40 continuous
minutes that clears all three checks, as a start time plus when it closes and
**what** closes it — "Head out any time up to 7:10 PM — after that you wouldn't
be back before dark." If nothing is left today, it gives you the first window
tomorrow morning instead.

Both panels are fed by an hourly forecast for 77006. Nothing is typed in
anywhere. A **Refresh** button pulls a current forecast and both panels update
in place without the page moving, and the panel always shows the time the
forecast it is reading was pulled.

The three checks, all measured across the whole 40 minutes rather than the
moment you press:

- **Rain** — any expected during the walk is a no.
- **Heat** — feels-like above 90°F is a no; 85–90°F is the short-one-and-shade
  middle answer; below that is fine.
- **Daylight** — the walk has to finish before sunset.

Heat and rain are read at their **worst** point across the walk, not at the
start: a walk that begins at 88°F and ends at 93°F is a walk that got too hot,
and the dog was out for all of it.

## Built differently
**Daylight is checked at both ends, not just sunset.** The spec asks that the
walk finish before sunset. Applied on its own that also calls 4am a fine time
to go out, and it would answer "the first window tomorrow morning" with
midnight. The whole walk now has to sit between sunrise and sunset, which is
what "dark before we finish" actually means.

**"Any rain expected" reads two numbers, not one.** A forecast says it might
rain in two different ways — a chance, and an amount — and each misses what the
other catches. Zero millimetres at 60% is a forecast saying a storm may pass
over; a light amount at 20% is one saying it will drizzle on you either way.
Either counts as rain.

**There is a Refresh button, and the panel says what time its forecast is
from.** The forecast has to be pulled at some point, and the honest thing is to
show you when that was rather than imply the answer is live. Pressing Refresh
makes it current and both panels update together. It is one press, and the
alternative — an answer that silently ages — is the one thing a dashboard like
this must not do.

## Open
**The verdict is "as of" the last refresh, not live.** A dashboard is handed the
day but never the time (`DashboardProps` in `lib/dashboard/contract.ts`), and
every question this screen asks is about a position in the day. So the
reference instant is the last successful refresh, shown on screen. That is
honest and it is one press from correct, but it is not the "in one look" the
spec describes.

The fix is small and is a platform decision, not a dashboard one: add
`now: number` to `DashboardProps`, resolved once per request in
`app/[user]/page.tsx`, which already calls `Date.now()` for `today`. It touches
every dashboard's contract, which is why it was flagged rather than done.
`queries.ts` already takes the minute as a parameter, so exactly one line in
`dashboard.tsx` changes if it lands.

**The 90°F cutoff may make this dashboard say no all summer.** Checked against a
real August forecast for 77006, feels-like sat above the no-go line for the
entire working day and the only qualifying window was about twenty minutes
around dawn. The numbers came from a suggestion accepted without a strong
opinion, and the spec itself says they are worth revisiting. A dashboard that
always says no stops being opened, so this wants asking before a few weeks pass
rather than after: would he rather the no-go line sat higher, or the
short-and-shady band stretched further up? `THRESHOLDS` in `queries.ts` is one
object and either is a one-line change — but it is his dog, and not a guess to
make on his behalf.

## Notes for the next build
**Local time is resolved at WRITE time and stored.** Every forecast row carries
the friend's local day and local minute-of-day, filled by the refresh route
from the `stairwell_tz` cookie. `queries.ts` therefore contains no timezone
logic at all and does not import `dayKey` — every check is integer arithmetic
on minutes since midnight. Do not "simplify" this by recomputing local time
from `at` at read time: that re-files old rows the first time he opens this from
another country, and the hard direction (wall clock to instant) then appears in
the middle of the window arithmetic.

**`forecast_hours` and `forecast_days` are replaced wholesale on every
successful refresh; `forecast_fetches` is appended to.** A forecast is a
snapshot, not a history — leaving old hours behind lets a window scan walk off
the end of what the provider currently stands behind. The fetch log is the
exception because the panel needs to tell "the refresh failed" from "there has
never been a refresh"; without that row a failed refresh is invisible and the
previous forecast renders as if it were current.

**`PLACES` in `app/api/users/[user]/forecast/route.ts` is a security boundary,
not a lookup convenience.** `lib/weather/openMeteo.ts` guarantees that nothing
about a friend reaches the provider; coordinates arriving in a request body
would break that immediately and turn the route into an open proxy. A slug with
no entry is a 404.

**The forecast client is pinned by a hand-written fixture plus an opt-in live
test.** `npm run test:live` is the only thing that would notice Open-Meteo
changing a field name — the fixture would keep passing while the panel said "no
forecast yet" forever. That script and the `*.live.test.ts` exclusion in
`vitest.config.ts` were added with this build and are the repo's first; they
never run in Gate E or a deploy, by design.

**`GoodWindow.closedBy` carries the whole failing reading, not a flag.** It
replaced an `endsAtDark` boolean during review, which could only ever explain
one of the three ways a window closes. A twenty-minute window is legitimate in a
Houston August, and naming its end time without the reason reads as a glitch.

**`001_initial.sql` stops being editable the moment this deploys.** Until his
first unlock applies it, it can still be changed freely; after that a change is
`002` plus a data-survival test.

**Two thresholds are load-bearing in different places.** The verdict uses both
90°F and 85°F; the window scan uses only 90°F, deliberately — the spec defines a
window as "heat index below 90°F", so a short-one-and-shade stretch still counts
as a window worth naming. They are not the same rule and should not be
collapsed.
